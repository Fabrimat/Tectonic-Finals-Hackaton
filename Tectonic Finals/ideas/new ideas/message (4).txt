# Ripple — customer journey
### From “Could this work for me?” to “Everything is coordinated.”

**Ripple** helps customers discover a suitable investment moment, understand its financial and environmental impact, and choose a complete partner solution without comparing and coordinating every provider themselves.

For the demo, the journey focuses on solar panels.

## 1. Discover — a relevant opportunity

The customer receives a short, explainable card:

> “Your loan ends in two months. Would you like to explore using some of that freed-up budget to lower your energy costs?”

Buttons: `Explore solar` · `Remind me later` · `Not interested`

The card explains which information triggered it. An ending loan is an invitation to explore, not proof that the customer should invest.

## 2. Personalise — one short intake

Ripple asks only for missing information:

- Property ownership and permission to install.
- Location and available roof details.
- Annual electricity consumption and typical usage pattern.
- Existing solar installation.
- Available budget and minimum savings buffer.
- Preferred installation period.

The customer chooses what matters most:

`Lower total cost` · `Lower upfront payment` · `Lower estimated climate impact`

Output: a reusable project profile, with confirmed, estimated and missing information clearly separated.

## 3. Understand — see the impact before deciding

One dashboard compares doing nothing, an individual offer and an available collective offer.

| View | What the customer sees |
|---|---|
| Money | Upfront cost, estimated annual energy benefit, financing payments and remaining buffer. |
| Energy | Estimated solar generation, electricity used directly at home and electricity exported. |
| Climate | Estimated emissions impact compared with the stated electricity baseline, including solar lifecycle emissions. |

For generation estimates, a production model can use location, system size, orientation and other installation assumptions. The European Commission’s PVGIS provides monthly and annual solar-production estimates for this purpose. [joint-research-centre.ec.europa](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis_en)

### Make the visuals easy to understand

Use three tabs rather than one confusing combined score:

- My money: cashflow with and without the project.
- My energy: monthly generation, direct consumption and grid imports.
- My climate impact: estimated cumulative emissions difference over time.

Every view has an `Assumptions and sources` button. Climate impact must not treat panels as emissions-free: manufacturing, transport, installation and end-of-life also matter. [iea-pvps](https://iea-pvps.org/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-environmental-life-cycle-assessment-of-electricity-from-pv-systems/)

## 4. Choose — two suitable packages, not twenty quotes

Ripple presents up to two complete proposals.

| Package | Example positioning |
|---|---|
| Individual route | An available individual offer with its own price and installation window. |
| Collective route | A partner’s group offer, with participation conditions and a defined installation window. |

Each proposal shows:

- Why it fits the customer’s situation.
- Equipment and installation scope.
- Total price and possible additional costs.
- Financial, energy and climate estimates.
- Warranty and service conditions.
- What still requires a technical check.

A package may explicitly exclude an unnecessary component:

> “No battery included: the current model does not establish sufficient additional value for your situation.”

Group discounts appear only when supported by a concrete partner offer. Demo prices are labelled fictional.

## 5. Join — contribute to collective demand

The customer can choose:

`Request this offer` · `Join the collective offer` · `Save for later`

With permission, Ripple groups comparable requests by region, installation category and preferred timeframe.

The partner receives a limited demand overview and can submit price, capacity and conditions. Joining a group expresses interest; it does not automatically create a purchase commitment.

Output: a clear status, such as:

> “Interest registered. Your personal offer will follow after the required checks.”

## 6. Approve — one prepared project dossier

Ripple reuses confirmed information to prepare:

- The partner request.
- Supporting property and consumption documents.
- The selected proposal.
- Any requested financing documents.
- A checklist of unresolved technical questions.

The customer approves the relevant actions separately:

- Share necessary information.
- Request the technical assessment.
- Accept the final offer.
- Submit a financing application, if wanted.

“One coordinated journey” does not mean one click silently accepts every contract.

## 7. Follow — one place to track execution

The customer sees:

> Profile complete → offer requested → technical check → final offer → customer approval → installation → completion

Each step shows who is responsible, what is missing and what happens next.

Partners receive the information needed for their task, not access to the customer’s banking history.

## 8. Review — compare expectations with results

After installation, Ripple can show actual production and financial outcomes where suitable data is connected with permission.

Keep the distinction clear:

- Measured: electricity generated.
- Calculated: bill impact using available consumption and tariff data.
- Modelled: avoided emissions relative to a defined baseline.

Do not label all three “verified savings”.

## The three models behind the experience

| Model | Main inputs | Main outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Offer price, payment timing, financing terms, tariffs and estimated energy flows. | Cashflow, remaining buffer and estimated payback. |
| Energy | Location, panel capacity, roof assumptions and consumption profile. | Generation, self-consumption, exports and grid imports. |
| Climate | Energy output, a sourced electricity-emissions baseline and solar lifecycle assumptions. | Estimated net emissions impact over a stated period. |

For the climate model, explain the baseline and method. A simple annual-average electricity factor is a screening assumption, not a precise claim about which power plants are displaced at each moment. Avoided-emissions results depend on location, timing and the electricity being displaced. [watttime](https://watttime.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/WattTime-SolarPVAvoidedAndEmbodiedEmissionsAnalysis-Report-202002-FINAL.pdf)

## The 90-second demo

1. A customer receives a relevant invitation.
2. A short intake confirms the situation and priorities.
3. Money, energy and climate views explain the options.
4. The customer selects a suitable collective offer.
5. Ripple generates the dossier and requests specific approvals.
6. Customer and partner see the same project progressing through their respective views.

> “Ripple turns a possible investment into an informed choice and a coordinated project — showing what it could mean for your money, your energy use and your climate impact.”
