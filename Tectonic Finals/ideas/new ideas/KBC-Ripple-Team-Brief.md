**KBC Ripple — From an early signal to a completed energy project.**

Updated working concept and implementation brief for the Tectonic finals, 20 October 2026. This revision incorporates all earlier material, the three new idea/transcript attachments, the latest team/mentor discussion and the subsequent `kbc ask.aac.txt` conversation about ecosystem value and partner choice. It is a recommendation for the team, not evidence that implementation or market validation has been completed.

Ripple helps households prepare and carry out their energy transition over time. It connects the customer's priorities, available money, technical checks and partners in one plan, and updates that plan when circumstances change.

**1. What changes, and what stays**

The team now prioritises private customers. The earlier brief recommended a catering-business journey; that was my recommendation, not an agreed team decision. This revision follows the new audience choice while retaining the earlier execution logic. The catering example and its monetary dataset are superseded for today's prototype. Business customers remain a possible second segment; they do not require a second demo.

The retained product mechanism is: identify a relevant signal, confirm the customer's goal, coordinate a shared plan, react to changes and check the outcome. The new material improves the timing and the household experience around that mechanism.

**2. Audience, promise and scope**

Primary audience: homeowners in Flanders who want to improve their home's energy performance and need help deciding when and how to proceed. This is a deliberate starting segment, not a verified claim that it is KBC's largest segment or the only scalable one.

The broader direction is ecological transition, including buildings and potentially mobility. Today's proof of concept uses one home and one solar project. It demonstrates a reusable approach without attempting every kind of renovation.

Customer promise: “Understand your next useful step, see what it means for your money, and keep the whole project coordinated.”

For KBC, the proposed commercial link is explicit: a confirmed, well-prepared customer project can lead to useful partner-service activity and, where the customer needs and chooses it, a financing or insurance review. Track those stages separately from an actual activated banking product.

Bank positioning: a proposed capability within the KBC/Kate/MyHome experience. Build a standalone prototype with simulated integrations. A public partner service does not give our team access to its systems.

We retain a planning horizon of up to five years where useful. We do not predict that someone will buy panels, replace a boiler or choose a car at a specific future date. We identify opportunities and constraints, then ask the customer what matters to them.

**3. The actual differentiation**

KBC already describes renovation planning, cost estimates through Setle, contractor support through Impact Us Today, energy insights with Eliq and MyHome's energy test [R1]. Its solar service already includes savings estimates, contractor requests and installation tracking [R2]. Gas/electricity group buying through Samen Overstappen is also already available [R3].

A calculator, an energy dashboard, a partner map, a group-buying button or a prepared dossier alone is therefore an insufficient differentiation claim.

The proposed addition is a persistent plan that connects three things:

- Preparation before the customer is ready to buy, with visible sources and customer-selected timing.
- Decisions that depend on each other: technical suitability, money, current offers, permissions and partner availability.
- Replanning and evidence after a change: what is affected, what must wait, what needs new approval and whether the intended result was achieved.

The decisive prototype moment is a technical finding that increases the project cost. Ripple updates the cash constraint, adds the required work, changes the schedule and requests fresh decisions. This is the earlier Mission/Ripple mechanism, now demonstrated within the chosen energy journey.

This is a differentiation hypothesis, not proof that no internal KBC system already does it. Ask the mentor about this exact cross-provider behaviour. Existing capabilities are permitted building blocks under the challenge.

**4. Preparation over time, without pretending to know the future**

Use simple customer labels: “Explore”, “Prepare” and “Act”. The internal Whisper/Forming/Ready labels from Claude can map to them. Time alone never moves a customer to “Ready”. Facts, suitability and explicit decisions do.

| Stage | Illustrative horizon | Useful behaviour | What does not happen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explore | Months to several years; up to five where useful | Explain a known date or observed cost trend; offer an optional energy-data connection or a review reminder | No assumed purchase intent, product commitment or partner sharing |
| Prepare | Often months before a chosen project | Confirm ownership, priorities, consumption, budget and technical questions; prepare comparable options | No claim that the first estimate is a binding quote or an affordability approval |
| Act | When the necessary checks and decisions are ready | Obtain current offers, share necessary information, approve commitments, coordinate delivery and track evidence | No automatic acceptance when a timer expires |

A customer can skip a stage, pause or move back after a change. A household may need a short journey; a business may need years. Remove the claim that small businesses generally have a five-year life or always transition within six to twelve months.

Example invitation: “Your current loan is scheduled to end in two months. Would you like to review whether an energy project fits your priorities and budget?” Buttons: `Explore` / `Remind me later` / `Not interested`.

The ending repayment is a known scheduling fact. It is not automatically disposable income, evidence of a renovation obligation or permission to sell a product. Confirm other obligations and the customer's intended use of the money.

The customer chooses reminder timing. A suggested future review and a materially changed fact can be useful; repeated sales prompts are not the product. Explain the reason for every invitation. Do not secretly steer someone for years while hiding the goal of the messages.

The energy feature analyses only the data authorised for that purpose. Goal confirmation, analysis permission, partner sharing and contract acceptance are separate decisions. This does not imply that turning the feature off stops unrelated ordinary banking processing.

**5. Signals, context and honest limits**

| Input | What it can support | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Known loan maturity | A future change in scheduled repayments | Money available for a project, consent or a need for solar |
| Energy bill in euros | A spending trend | A consumption trend in kWh; tariffs and billing adjustments also matter |
| Authorised interval consumption data | A more informed usage profile | Roof suitability or all solar generation behind the meter |
| Confirmed property details and assessment | Technical questions and suitable next checks | A guaranteed installation outcome before inspection |
| Confirmed EPC and relevant legal facts | A possible need to check a renovation deadline | That solar alone meets the obligation |
| Boiler age or a repair invoice | A reason to ask about maintenance and plans | The exact failure date or a mandatory replacement |
| Area or heritage information | A reason to check applicable restrictions | A rule that all homes in Bruges are blocked or that Mechelen has no restrictions |

Real Fluvius access requires an appropriate data-access arrangement and customer authorisation; it is not established by a mock “Connect” button [R4]. In the demo, use synthetic readings and explicitly simulated connections.

If an EPC deadline is shown, establish the exact property and deed facts first. The current Flemish guidance describes a six-year period for qualifying transfers of E/F-rated homes, with exceptions and specific rules, including remaining deadlines on subsequent transfers [R7]. The financial loan date must not substitute for the legal deed date. The solar demo does not claim to fulfil a renovation obligation.

Grant and low-emission-zone claims from earlier material are excluded from the main demo. They need their own current official eligibility checks before use. Do not use the end of a grant to manufacture urgency or assume every household qualifies.

**6. One customer and one complete project**

Sara owns a home in Ghent. She has opted into energy insights. An explainable invitation leads her to consider a solar project as one step in her longer-term energy plan. A compressed, clearly labelled demo timeline shows earlier preparation, current decision-making and later follow-up. It is not a record of a real multi-year prediction.

The timeline has three illustrative checkpoints: three years earlier, a known future repayment end prompts an optional review reminder; six months before the intended project, Sara confirms her priorities and supplies missing consumption/property information; today, she compares current offers. The earlier checkpoints do not lock in a product or predict her eventual decision. The ending repayment never becomes project cash automatically.

Her confirmed priorities are lower energy costs, understandable environmental impact and keeping her selected cash buffer. She can choose another priority or decline the project.

The short intake asks only for missing information: ownership and installation permission, location, roof assumptions, existing equipment, consumption profile, money allocated to the plan, other relevant commitments and preferred timing. Every important field is labelled confirmed, estimated or missing, with its source and date.

The prototype journey is:

1. Show the signal and why it is relevant. Sara chooses whether to explore.
2. Confirm her goal and constraints. Display missing facts instead of inventing them.
3. Compare the baseline without this project and up to two comparable offers: individual and collective, if a relevant collective offer exists. Show a recommended route with reasons and a meaningful alternative where available; one suitable option is acceptable when the limitation is explained.
4. Sara selects an option for investigation and authorises the specific technical-check request. This does not accept an installation contract.
5. The technical assessment reports additional electrical work. The cost, sequence and timing of the whole plan change.
6. Ripple explains the consequence and offers genuine choices: wait and save, request a revised suitable scope, seek a financing consultation or stop. It does not promise every alternative will be feasible.
7. In the chosen demo path, Sara elects to wait. Only an actual simulated receipt, current partner confirmation and fresh customer approvals allow the paid work to proceed.
8. The electrical work is completed before the dependent installation step. Installation, commissioning/checklist evidence, payments and customer acceptance are recorded separately.
9. Mark the project “Completed — demo” only when its required evidence is present. Start a later results review with measured, calculated and modelled values clearly distinguished.

A multi-year plan does not require the customer to make every decision today. The interface always shows one useful next action and the wider plan behind it.

Keep one optional banking-service action in this same journey: `Request a KBC financing review`. If Sara chooses it, prepare the current quote, requested amount, timing and customer-confirmed relevant information; show the recipient and fields before sharing. Record “Review requested — demo”, not “Loan approved”. An insurance review is relevant only when the project and existing coverage justify it; do not assert that solar installation necessarily requires a new policy. The main demonstrated path remains waiting and using additional own savings. It creates no new-product conversion, and the demo must count it honestly.

This is a customer constraint, not a certified safe reserve. The prototype must explain the simplified coverage; real affordability needs the rest of the household situation.

**8. Money, energy and climate: three clear views**

Retain the three views from the new journey. Avoid combining euros, kWh and emissions into an unexplained score. Every view exposes its assumptions and distinguishes doing nothing, the individual offer and the collective offer.

| View | Minimum honest output | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| My money | Project payments, remaining buffer, baseline comparison and an estimated operating benefit | A loan repayment is not an energy saving; grants and future receipts are not available money |
| My energy | Generation, direct household use, exports and grid imports | Annual consumption alone does not establish hourly self-consumption |
| My climate impact | A sourced model and stated baseline; otherwise “Not yet calculated” with missing inputs | Modelled avoided emissions are not a measured result |

PVGIS is an appropriate source for location- and configuration-dependent solar-production estimates [R5]. It does not establish roof safety, a personalised tariff, financing eligibility or the customer's self-consumption from an annual bill alone. A future integration must retain inputs, retrieval date and model version. Do not label hand-entered numbers “PVGIS results”.

For a deterministic offline energy illustration, use these explicitly synthetic assumptions:

- Annual household electricity use: 4,000 kWh.
- Annual solar generation: 4,000 kWh.
- Direct solar use: 1,600 kWh; exports: 2,400 kWh; remaining grid imports: 2,400 kWh.
- Avoided variable import cost: EUR 0.30/kWh; export remuneration: EUR 0.04/kWh.
- Gross annual operating benefit: 1,600 × 0.30 + 2,400 × 0.04 = EUR 576.
- Illustrative annual maintenance allowance: EUR 80; simplified annual operating benefit: EUR 496.

These are scenario inputs, not a performance forecast for Sara's actual roof. Show fixed charges and costs outside this model as unchanged or excluded; do not multiply all generation by the retail price. Do not credit operating benefits before commissioning. A delay moves the benefit timeline; use a seasonal production profile before claiming a precise lost benefit for a particular month.

A static simple-payback illustration is about 13.1 years at EUR 6,500 or 14.9 years at EUR 7,400 using EUR 496 per year. It excludes financing, discounting, degradation, major replacements and changing tariffs. A five-year planning horizon does not imply five-year payback. Keep payback accessible in the money view with its assumptions. The short pitch can focus on coordination, while the customer still sees this information before deciding.

For climate, choose a defensible electricity baseline and include solar lifecycle impacts. IEA PVPS explicitly assesses impacts across the lifecycle [R6]. Do not invent a Belgian emissions factor, call panels emissions-free, count exports twice or claim that buying a green contract proves an equal additional physical emissions reduction. If a defensible model is not ready, keep the climate view informative without a fabricated numerical result.

After installation, distinguish: production measured by an appropriate production source; bill impact calculated using available flows and tariffs; emissions impact modelled against a counterfactual. Grid import/export data alone does not reveal all behind-the-meter production. Short-term readings are not proof of annual savings. Demo follow-up readings remain synthetic.

A battery is excluded from the initial scope. Say “Additional value has not been established for this scenario” if that is what the evidence supports. Remove the rule that every plan must contain a “not needed” card. An honest recommendation is conditional, not a compulsory sales technique.

**10. Minimum build and rules that carry over**

Build three primary views:

| View | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| Customer workspace | Early signal, source explanation, intake, time horizon, choices, cash buffer, shared action plan, change review, approvals and outcome |
| Partner workspace | Only the assigned service request; permitted fields; assessment/quote response; changed terms; task confirmation and completion |
| Demo controls | Clearly labelled time advances, cash receipts, technical findings, partner confirmations and completion evidence; isolated reset |

Show two partner roles in the same workspace framework: the assessor/electrical-work provider and the solar installer. Their steps must affect the customer's shared state. A map can remain one pitch illustration rather than another application screen.

Real prototype behaviour: dated arithmetic, energy arithmetic, current-state checks, dependencies, versioned offers, approvals, field-level access and event-driven updates. Simulated behaviour: bank access, meter access, real quotes, payments, inspections, contracts and installations. State the distinction on screen and in the README.

Retain and adapt these rules from the previous brief:

- The server checks actor ownership, purpose-specific permission and current state before any external-action simulation.
- A non-binding enquiry is distinct from a paid assessment, an accepted quote and a booking. Make any assessment fee explicit before approval.
- All required costs remain in the forecast, including unconfirmed but necessary work. A declined provider does not make an essential expense disappear.
- Pending money belongs only in a conditional projection. A bank event supplies actual receipt evidence. Internal transfers between included accounts do not create additional wealth.
- Changed price, scope, provider or timing creates a new plan/offer version and requires the relevant fresh approvals, even when a substitute is cheaper.
- Offer expiry and partner availability are checked again after a delay. Do not silently reserve capacity.
- The electrical-work completion is a prerequisite for the dependent installation in this specific assessed scenario. Do not invent a universal installation sequence for all homes.
- Customer approval does not override a missing technical prerequisite or fabricate funds. Revising a customer constraint is a separate explicit decision, never an automatic fix.
- Duplicate clicks and repeated partner/payment events do not duplicate commitments or money.
- Withdrawn sharing permission stops future unauthorised sharing; it does not automatically cancel contracts or erase information already received by a partner.
- A partner cannot see balances, the reserve, other providers' offers or unrelated household records.
- Completion needs the project's agreed installation, commissioning/checklist, acceptance and payment evidence. “Request sent” is not a successful outcome.
- Record each event's source, time, plan/offer version and simulation status. Record unknown information as unknown.

Use one simple application and one state store. React/Node remain reasonable if familiar to the builders; avoid framework changes. Use integer cents, explicit dates, consistent same-day ordering and deterministic calculations. A small durable store is preferable if easy; isolated in-memory demo sessions are acceptable with a clear reset/restart limitation.

Useful records are Household, Property, Signal, Consent, Goal, ProjectVersion, Action, Offer, Approval, CashEvent and OutcomeEvidence. One set of dependency rules determines waiting, needs-review, needs-approval, blocked, confirmed and completed states.

AI may extract proposed fields from text or explain an already computed change. Confirm extracted facts, validate structure and restrict service identifiers. It must not determine cash arithmetic, infer legal eligibility, grant approval or execute an action from document instructions. Keep the whole journey functional with seeded data and clear template explanations before adding a live model call.

**12. Customer, KBC and partner value**

The new `kbc ask.aac.txt` conversation materially sharpens this section. Around 00:19–00:55, the KBC representative emphasises ecosystem usage, including partner activity and conversion to KBC's own products. Around 00:57–01:05, the speakers indicate that partner margins depend on the partner. Around 01:09–01:53, they allow partner choice and explain that one or several providers can be justified by the customer's problem. These are statements from the supplied discussion, not a complete official profitability policy or a confirmed commercial agreement. The unclear word transcribed as “COVID” is not used to identify a platform or channel.

| Party | Proposed benefit | Main evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Less coordination work, clearer choices, fewer unexpected commitments and an explicit buffer | Completed projects, time spent, budget/timing deviations, unresolved issues and satisfaction |
| KBC | Meaningful ecosystem use and conversion to suitable KBC products when customers need and choose them | Partner-service requests and completed services; separate financing/insurance enquiry, application and actual product-activation stages |
| Partners | Better prepared enquiries, clearer scope and timing, fewer failed appointments | Enquiry-to-accepted-offer and enquiry-to-completed-service conversion, handling effort and cancellations |

For KBC, present usage and suitable product conversion as the principal business measures supported by this discussion. Retention, service efficiency and any partner remuneration are additional hypotheses or agreement-dependent benefits. Do not lead with guaranteed referral commissions.

Use a small event funnel behind the demo, not another large dashboard:

| Stage | Counted event | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Customer engagement | Customer chooses to explore the invitation | A purchase decision |
| Partner use | Authorised service request sent; distinguish an accepted request and delivered service | Completed work from a click alone |
| Banking-service interest | Customer explicitly requests a KBC consultation or review | A credit application or insurance sale |
| Application | A properly submitted application in a real future integration | Approval or activation |
| Product conversion | The defined completed product event, such as a disbursed loan or an activated policy | That every preceding contact caused the sale |
| Customer outcome | Project completed with the required evidence and approved constraints | Verified annual energy savings or measured avoided emissions |

Specify the denominator and observation window for every rate. For example, financing application-to-disbursement conversion is disbursed loans divided by submitted applications in a defined cohort; do not compare it with a rate using all app visitors as the denominator. Compare with the existing journey before claiming an incremental improvement. Track withdrawals and unsuitable recommendations as well as successes.

Every prototype event carries a simulation label. Demo counts illustrate instrumentation, not market traction. In Sara's main own-funds path, new banking-product conversions are zero. An optional review request counts only at the review-request stage. The business case concerns a portfolio of suitable journeys, not selling a new product to every household.

Revenue is not guaranteed. Referral remuneration depends on actual agreements and must be disclosed. Leads do not have zero acquisition or servicing cost. Do not require a loan to make every journey commercially useful or rank a worse option higher because it pays more commission. Self-funding, deferral and stopping remain real choices.

A later business case should measure contribution per completed project: incremental banking-product contribution plus any contracted partner/service income minus data, integration, support and operating costs. Conversion is not itself profit. Retention is a hypothesis to test, not immediate cash revenue. The customer's project price is not KBC revenue.

The primary customer-outcome metric is the share of eligible, opted-in projects completed within their latest approved budget and timing, with the selected reserve respected. Report energy readings, calculated bill impact and modelled emissions separately. No improvement percentage has been established.
